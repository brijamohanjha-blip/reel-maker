# Writing the voiceover script

## Structure (30-50 s)

| Beat | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | Stop the scroll in the first 2 s: a myth, a sharp question, or a surprising (verifiable) fact. Name the topic in the first sentence. | 2-4 s |
| 2 Tension | Why it matters / what most people get wrong. Opens a loop the payoff closes. | 3-6 s |
| 3-5 Value | One idea per beat, concrete and visual (each beat needs a clip you can picture). | 4-8 s each |
| 6 Payoff | Answer the hook plainly, including the honest limits ("lekin…"). | 3-6 s |
| 7 Close | Practical next step, e.g. "dermatologist se milo" for health, or a soft save/share line. | 2-4 s |

Use 5-8 beats. A new beat every 3-8 s keeps the cuts frequent, which is what holds attention.

## What makes it shareable (without overclaiming)

- **Hook formulas**: "X sach mein kaam karta hai, ya sirf hype?" · "Y karna band karo, jab tak yeh nahi jaante" · "Z ke baare mein 90% log galat sochte hain" (only with a real, checkable basis) · "Ek cheez jo koi nahi batata…".
- **Specific beats vague**: name the mechanism, the time frame, the comparison.
- **One surprise per Reel**: the fact people repeat to a friend. It must be the most solid fact on the sheet.
- **Honest limits build trust**: "Poora tareeka abhi puri tarah saaf nahi hai" performs better than hype and keeps it safe.
- **End on something useful**, not "follow for more" (unless the user asks for a CTA).

## Health and skin language

- Hedge mechanism and outcome claims: "ho sakta hai", "maana jaata hai", "research suggests", "kai logon mein".
- Never: cures, permanent, guaranteed, 100%, dosages, "doctor ki zaroorat nahi".
- Mention side effects or limits where they exist ("band karne par fayda ruk sakta hai").
- No first-person experiences ("maine try kiya") for an AI voice.

## ElevenLabs formatting

- Spoken words only: no stage directions, emojis, hashtags, beat labels or brackets.
- Numbers and units as words ("chaar mahine", not "4 mahine"). Spell out abbreviations the way they should be said ("B P" or "blood pressure").
- Punctuation controls pacing: commas for small breaths, full stops between ideas, "…" for a dramatic pause, "?" for real rising intonation.
- Keep sentences short (under ~18 words). English technical words inside Hinglish are fine in Roman script ("enzyme", "growth phase").
- Hindi words in Roman script, spelled the way they're pronounced; keep spellings consistent across the script (captions show this exact text).
- Never mention AI, voice generation or tools inside the script.

## Fact sheet format

```
1. Claim: "<exact sentence from the script>"
   Check: <the precise thing to confirm, e.g. "Minoxidil was first approved as an oral blood-pressure drug">
   Settle it with: <source type: drug label / regulator (FDA, CDSCO), dermatology association (AAD, IADVL), systematic review on PubMed>
   Confidence: high / medium / unsure — <why>
```

Never invent a study, statistic, URL or quote. If a claim needs a number you're not sure of, say so and offer number-free wording.
