# Writing the voiceover script

A generic script lists tips. An engaging one makes **one specific person** feel seen in the first second, keeps a question open until the end, and leaves the viewer thinking of someone to send it to. Work through the steps in order. Don't skip to writing lines.

## Why Reels get reach (design for this)

- **Sends beat likes.** A DM share is the strongest signal for pushing a Reel to non-followers. Build every script so the natural reaction is "I should send this to ___".
- **The first 3 seconds decide.** Drop-off before 3 s caps distribution. No warm-up, no "aaj hum baat karenge", no greeting.
- **Many people watch muted.** The hook must work as on-screen text and visuals alone. Word-by-word captions carry the rest.
- **Topic legibility.** A stranger should know what the Reel is about from the first frame and the caption's first line (keywords, not hashtags, drive search).

## Step 1: Brief (fill silently from the idea; ask only if a line is impossible)

```
TOPIC + KEYWORD PHRASE:  the words a stranger would search ("winter dry skin routine")
NAMED VIEWER:            one specific person and moment ("the person whose cheeks feel tight by noon in October")
SEND TARGET:             who the viewer will forward it to ("the friend who uses the same foaming face wash all year")
LENS:                    the non-obvious angle (Step 2)
PAYOFF:                  the one thing they can do or say after watching
ONE SURPRISE:            the most solid, checkable fact that people repeat to a friend
```

## Step 2: Pick the lens before writing

The topic is shared by thousands of creators. The lens makes it yours. List the obvious angle, then push past it:

- **Invert the villain.** The obvious villain (weather, hard water, pollution) usually isn't the one the viewer controls. What is? ("Mausam nahi, tumhara face wash.")
- **Find the habit.** Name the exact routine behaviour that causes the problem (the squeaky-clean feeling, the summer gel moisturiser still in use in November).
- **Second-order effect.** Not the event, what it quietly causes (dry air → barrier → products that never stung now sting).
- **Switch POV.** From the skin's side, the friend's side, the dermatologist's chair.
- **Flip the win.** What feels like a win is the cost ("jo 'squeaky clean' feel tumhe pasand hai, wahi tightness hai").

## Step 3: Write three hook systems, then choose

Write **three** options from different archetypes, then pick the strongest (show all three to the user, recommend one). Each option has three layers that do **different jobs**:

| Layer | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| First frame (visual) | Shows the conflict, stake or payoff before any word | Describe it for the clip search; no slow fade-in |
| Spoken line | The why/how behind it | 5-12 words, the most interesting word early, one claim |
| On-screen text (`onScreenText`) | The what, compressed | 3-6 words, sharpens rather than repeats the voice |

**Archetypes to draw from** (vary across the three options):

- **Named viewer / self-identification:** "Agar October aate hi tumhare gaal noon tak kheenchne lagte hain…"
- **Contrarian (only if the body proves it):** "Is mausam mein tumhari skin ko zyada cleaning nahi, kam cleaning chahiye."
- **Investigator:** "Tumhare bathroom mein ek cheez hai jo is mausam mein skin ko sukha rahi hai."
- **Mistake / habit callout (never attack the viewer):** "Summer wala moisturiser November mein bhi? Yahi galti sabse common hai."
- **Stakes + specific number (only if true):** "Hawa ki nami aadhi, aur routine wahi ka wahi."
- **Myth vs fact:** "'Sardi mein sunscreen ki zaroorat nahi.' Sach kya hai?"

Hook rules: never insult the viewer (pull them in through a shared situation); promise a concrete payoff, not homework; no vague tease ("aage jo hoga…") without naming what kind of answer is coming.

## Step 4: The body is a value-tease ladder

Each beat gives **part** of the answer and opens the **next** question. Join beats with **"lekin" (but)** or **"isliye/toh" (therefore)**, never "aur phir / pehla, doosra, teesra" piling.

- Weak (list): "Pehla, mild cleanser. Doosra, rich moisturiser. Teesra, sunscreen."
- Strong (ladder): "Tightness ka pehla culprit tumhara face wash hai… **lekin** sirf wash badalna kaafi nahi, kyunki dry hawa nami kheench leti hai… **isliye** moisturiser ka timing bhi badlo… **aur sabse badi galti** wo hai jo log thand aate hi chhod dete hain."
- Add a **rehook** between 3 and 10 s: a turn ("lekin…"), a surprise, or a question that resets attention.
- Give lists a **memorable name** so they're shareable: "October wala 3-step reset", not "3 tips".
- Specific beats generic: an instance, time, place or sensation ("dopahar tak gaal kheenchna", "wash ke baad 'squeaky' feel") instead of a category ("skin dry ho jaati hai").
- Vary sentence length. Short. Then a longer line that builds and lands. Then short again.
- One idea per beat, and every beat must be picturable (it becomes one clip).

## Step 5: Ending (write it before the middle)

- **Last line = shareable "last dab":** a line someone would repeat. Make it hand off back into the hook so the loop replays naturally.
- **Send-prompt CTA, aimed at a named persona:** "Ye us dost ko bhejo jo saal bhar ek hi foaming face wash use karta hai." Never "follow for more" or "like karo".
- Health topics keep a safety line ("problem lambi chale toh dermatologist se milo"), but put it **before** the send-prompt or fold it into the payoff so the Reel doesn't end on a disclaimer.

## Step 6: Health and skin language (non-negotiable)

- Be specific about **situations and behaviours**; hedge **outcomes and mechanisms**: "ho sakta hai", "madad kar sakta hai", "maana jaata hai", "kai logon mein".
- Never: cures, permanent, guaranteed, 100%, dosages, "doctor ki zaroorat nahi", invented numbers or studies.
- A contrarian hook must be defensible by a reputable source; if not, use a softer archetype.
- No first-person testimonials or "maine try kiya" for an AI voice.

## Step 7: Length and ElevenLabs formatting

- Budget ~2.8 words/s (or the user's measured rate): 30 s ≈ 80 words, 40 s ≈ 110, 50 s ≈ 135. Cut beats, never the lens, the surprise or the last line.
- Spoken words only: no beat labels, emojis, hashtags or brackets. Numbers and units as words ("chaar hafte").
- Punctuation is pacing: commas for breath, full stops between ideas, "…" before a reveal, "?" for real questions.
- Roman-script Hinglish, spelled as pronounced, consistent throughout (captions show this exact text). English skincare words stay English ("moisturiser", "barrier").

## Step 8: Audit before delivering (rewrite anything that fails)

Score each 0-2 (16 max, deliver only at 13+; show the score):

1. **Viewer:** does the first line make one specific person think "this is me"?
2. **Three layers:** do frame, voice and on-screen text each do a different job, and does the hook work muted?
3. **Open loop:** is there unfinished business the viewer needs resolved, with a rehook before 10 s?
4. **Ladder:** but/therefore between beats, zero "pehla/doosra/aur phir" piling?
5. **Specificity:** at least two concrete instances (time, sensation, habit) instead of categories?
6. **Lens and surprise:** is the angle non-obvious, and is there one fact worth repeating?
7. **Ending:** a repeatable last line plus a named-persona send-prompt?
8. **Safety and truth:** hedged outcomes, nothing invented, contrarian claims defensible?

Also cut: throat-clearing first sentences, "aaj hum baat karenge", "dosto", engagement bait ("comment karo agar…"), hype words ("game-changer", "magic"), and a second sentence that only repeats the first.

## Fact sheet format

```
1. Claim: "<exact sentence from the script>"
   Check: <the precise thing to confirm>
   Settle it with: <source type: drug label / regulator (FDA, CDSCO), dermatology association (AAD, IADVL), systematic review on PubMed, IMD for weather>
   Confidence: high / medium / unsure — <why>
```

Never invent a study, statistic, URL or quote. If a claim needs a number you're not sure of, say so and offer number-free wording.

## Post caption (deliver with every script)

- Line 1: the keyword phrase as a headline (search surface).
- 1-2 lines expanding the hook in plain language.
- The named-persona send-prompt.
- 3-5 relevant hashtags at most.
- The disclosure line last: "AI voice used. Not medical advice." (or "AI voice used." for non-health topics).
